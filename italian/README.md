# Italian course: roadmap

**Goal:** pass an **Italian proficiency exam at B1** (CILS, CELI, PLIDA or CERT.IT; see
**🎓 The exam** below) in about **6 months** at ~30 min/day, then keep going to B2 mostly
through listening, reading and conversation.

Italian is very close to Portuguese: most core words are recognisable, the 7 vowels are the same,
and the grammar works the same way most of the time. So, like the Spanish course, this roadmap
spends its time on **the differences**: the sounds a Brazilian accent changes, the articles and
plurals, *essere* vs *avere* in the past, *ci* and *ne*, and the subjunctive after "I think".

Each unit = **one rule family + ~15–20 high-frequency words + one output task**.
⚡ marks units that are almost identical to Portuguese: read the rule, do the drills, move on.

> 🌱 **Status: starter.** ✅ **Lesson pages: units 0–3** (marked ✅ below). The study app has decks
> for the same units and the Tutor knows the course and the exam. Generate the next lesson pages

## 🤔 Why it feels easy (and the trap)

- **Why it feels easy:** you understand a lot of Italian from day one, and Italians understand a
  lot of your Portuguese.
- **The trap: *portuliano*.** Because you're understood, it's easy to speak Portuguese with Italian
  endings and stop improving. Those mistakes *fossilise* (they become habits that are very hard to
  undo), and **an exam scores exactly those mistakes**. Study the differences on purpose.
- **Italian and Spanish leak into each other more than any other pair in this repo.** If you run
  both, follow the rules in the [Spanish roadmap](../spanish/README.md)'s *Running it alongside French*
  (separate slots, one language per session, a mix-up list). With an exam coming, pausing Spanish
  is the safer choice.

## 🎓 The exam

Four Italian certifications are recognised by the Italian state (together they form the **CLIQ**
group). All four follow the CEFR levels (A1–C2), and **B1** is the level usually asked for
Italian citizenship by marriage or residence. B2 is often asked by universities.

| Exam | Run by | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **CILS** | Università per Stranieri di Siena | Has a separate grammar section ("structures"); also a *B1 Cittadinanza* version |
| **CELI** | Università per Stranieri di Perugia | Levels are numbered: CELI 2 = B1, CELI 3 = B2 |
| **PLIDA** | Società Dante Alighieri | Often available in Brazil through the local *Dante Alighieri* societies |
| **CERT.IT** | Università Roma Tre | |

They all test the **same four skills**: **listening, reading, writing, speaking** (CILS adds a fifth
section for grammar). You usually have to **pass each section**, not only the total. Formats, dates
and fees change: check your exam's official site, and download its free **sample papers**
(*esempi di prove*).

How the course trains each part:

| Exam section | In this course |
|---|---|
| Listening | Every unit's **🎧 Input + Hunt**; podcasts and series below |
| Reading | **Tutor → Reading** mode with real paragraphs; unit 27 |
| Grammar / structures | The **📐 rule** + flashcards + self-tests of every unit |
| Writing | Every **🗣️ Use it** text → **Tutor → Corrector**; unit 25 for letters and emails |
| Speaking | **Tutor → Conversation** (choose the *examiner* scenario) and **Pronunciation** |

**Mock exams:** units 13 (A1), 23 (A2) and 28 (B1) are mock exams. In the last 6 weeks before the
test, do **one timed section per week** from the official sample papers, and send your writing to
the Corrector.

**Pronunciation in the exam:** a Brazilian accent is fine. What costs points is what changes words:
double consonants (*anno / ano*), final vowels (*-e* vs *-i*: *notte / notti*), and *ci / ti*.
Unit 0 covers all three.

## Notes
- 🔊 **Audio** uses an Italian (it-IT) voice. The same voice reads the Latin course.
- ⌨️ **Typing:** the accent keys have à è é ì ò ù. Accents only appear on a stressed final vowel
  (*città, perché*) and on *è* (is).
- 🌍 **Which Italian?** Standard Italian, the one exams use. Real Italians mix in regional accents
  (*casa* with z in the North, s in the South) and dialect words: the course marks them when they
  come up.
- **Resources:** *Podcast Italiano* (Davide Gemello: beginner episodes, then intermediate); *Easy
  Italian* (YouTube street interviews with subtitles); *Italiano Automatico* for B1. A textbook
  like *Nuovo Espresso* or *Nuovo Contatto* gives a printed spine if you want one.
- 🎬 **Series:** *Skam Italia* (clear, modern, everyday Italian) once you reach A2. *L'amica geniale*
  is great but has a lot of Neapolitan dialect: save it for B1.
- 🎵 **Music to start with** (clear diction): Laura Pausini, Andrea Bocelli, Jovanotti, Eros
  Ramazzotti. Rap and trap (e.g. Marracash, Sfera Ebbasta) are fast and full of slang: save them for
  the Song Workshop later.
