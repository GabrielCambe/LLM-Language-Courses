# Italian course: roadmap

**Goal:** pass the **CILS B1 Cittadinanza** exam (see **🎓 The exam** below) in about **6 months**
at ~30 min/day (units 0–26), then go on to **B2** with the B2 track (units 27–31).

Italian is very close to Portuguese: most core words are recognisable, the 7 vowels are the same,
and the grammar works the same way most of the time. So, like the Spanish course, this roadmap
spends its time on **the differences**: the sounds a Brazilian accent changes, the articles and
plurals, *essere* vs *avere* in the past, *ci* and *ne* and, for B2, the subjunctive after "I think".

Each unit = **one rule family + ~15–20 high-frequency words + one output task**.
⚡ marks units that are almost identical to Portuguese: read the rule, do the drills, move on.

> 🌱 **Status: starter.** ✅ **Lesson pages: units 0–4** (marked ✅ below). The study app has decks
> for the same units and the Tutor knows the course and the exam. Generate the next lesson pages
> with [`../docs/unit-template.md`](../docs/unit-template.md) when you reach them.

> 🧭 **Scope guard:** you only need the unit you're on and the next one. The exam has no date yet,

> exam day: the other courses pause or drop to flashcards only. The B2 track waits until after the

## 🤔 Why it feels easy (and the trap)

- **Why it feels easy:** you understand a lot of Italian from day one, and Italians understand a
  lot of your Portuguese.
- **The trap: *portuliano*.** Because you're understood, it's easy to speak Portuguese with Italian
  endings and stop improving. Those mistakes *fossilise* (they become habits that are very hard to
  undo), and **an exam scores exactly those mistakes**. Study the differences on purpose.
- **Italian and Spanish leak into each other more than any other pair in this repo.** If you run
  both, follow the rules in the [Spanish roadmap](../spanish/README.md)'s *Running it alongside French*
  (separate slots, one language per session, a mix-up list). With an exam coming, pausing Spanish
  is the safer choice.

## 🎓 The exam: CILS B1 Cittadinanza

The **CILS B1 Cittadinanza** is the citizenship version of the CILS B1, run by the Università per
Stranieri di Siena. Its topics are everyday life in Italy: personal life, public offices, work and
school. These figures come from the official syllabus, sample booklet and scoring criteria on the
[CILS B1 Cittadinanza page](https://cils.unistrasi.it/1/89/382/Prove_Liv._B1_c.htm) (checked
November 2026: formats can change, so check again before you book).

| Section | Tasks | Time | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🎧 Listening | 2: multiple choice (3 options, 6 questions) + true/false (12 statements). 400–700 words of audio in total, **played twice**, standard Italian at medium speed | ~30 min | 12 |
| 📖 Reading + grammar | 2: true/false on a text (12) + a multiple-choice cloze (fill the gaps, 6). Emails, news articles, notices, rules, instructions | 40 min | 12 |
| ✍️ Writing | 1 **email of 80–120 words**: you pick 1 of 2 topics (in the sample: write to your landlord because you want to move out, or to the head of your child's school about the canteen) | 40 min | 12 |
| 🗣️ Speaking | 1: a **~1-minute self-presentation** from a list of questions, then a **2–3-minute dialogue** with the examiner, giving and commenting on opinions | ~10 min | 12 |

Three rules shape the plan:
- **You must pass every section, and passed sections don't carry over.** In the standard CILS B1,
  passed sections stay valid for 18 months and you only retake the failed ones (*capitalizzazione*).
  In B1 Cittadinanza there's no *capitalizzazione*: failing one section means **redoing the whole
  exam**. No section can be a weak spot.
- **The certificate is valid only for citizenship.** The standard CILS UNO-B1 also counts for work
  and study. Results take about 2 months.
- **The grammar stops at B1:** present, *passato prossimo*, *imperfetto*, imperative, *condizionale
  presente*, infinitive; personal, reflexive and relative pronouns; articulated prepositions;
  comparatives. **No *congiuntivo*, no *passato remoto*.** That's why those are in the B2 track. In
  the dialogue, give opinions with *secondo me* + the indicative or *penso di* + an infinitive.
  That's correct Italian, and it avoids a tense you don't need yet.

How writing and speaking are scored (12 points each):
- **Writing:** communicative effectiveness 3, register 1, grammar 4, vocabulary 3, spelling and
  punctuation 1.
- **Speaking:** communicative effectiveness 4, grammar 4, vocabulary 3, **pronunciation and
  intonation 1**.

So a Brazilian accent costs at most 1 point. Errors that change the word (*anno / ano*,
*notte / notti*, *ci / ti*) still cost points, under effectiveness and grammar. Unit 0 covers all three.

How the course trains each section:

| Exam section | In this course |
|---|---|
| Listening | Every unit's **🎧 Input + Hunt**, podcasts (see Notes), and from unit 23 timed listening with two plays only |
| Reading + grammar | The **📐 rule**, flashcards and self-tests of every unit; **Tutor → Reading** mode with short practical texts (notices, rules, emails) |
| Writing | Every **🗣️ Use it** text → **Tutor → Corrector**; unit 24 is the 80–120-word email |
| Speaking | **Tutor → Conversation**, scenario *a CILS examiner* (1-minute presentation + opinion dialogue); unit 25 |

**Mock exams:** units 13 (A1), 23 (A2) and 26 (B1 Cittadinanza). The official page has a free
**sample booklet with the audio, transcripts, answer keys and scoring criteria**. Save it, unseen,
for unit 26: a mock you've already looked at measures your memory, not your Italian.

**Booking:** you register at a CILS exam centre (there are centres in Italy and abroad: see the
[list of centres](https://cils.unistrasi.it/1/84/16/Le_sedi_di_esami.htm)).

**And B2?** A B2 certificate (e.g. the standard CILS DUE-B2) is above the B1 the citizenship rule
asks for, and it's the level universities usually want. If you reach B2 before booking, ask whoever
handles your application (comune, prefettura or consulate) whether a B2 certificate is accepted
too. Then one exam would cover both goals.

## Notes
- 🔊 **Audio** uses an Italian (it-IT) voice. The same voice reads the Latin course.
- ⌨️ **Typing:** the accent keys have à è é ì ò ù. Accents only appear on a stressed final vowel
  (*città, perché*) and on *è* (is).
- 🌍 **Which Italian?** Standard Italian, the one exams use. Real Italians mix in regional accents
  (*casa* with z in the North, s in the South) and dialect words: the course marks them when they
  come up.
- **Resources:** *Podcast Italiano* (Davide Gemello: beginner episodes, then intermediate); *Easy
  Italian* (YouTube street interviews with subtitles); *Italiano Automatico* for B1. A textbook
  like *Nuovo Espresso* or *Nuovo Contatto* gives a printed spine if you want one.
- 🎬 **Series:** *Skam Italia* (clear, modern, everyday Italian) once you reach A2. *L'amica geniale*
  is great but has a lot of Neapolitan dialect: save it for B1.
- 🎵 **Music to start with** (clear diction): Laura Pausini, Andrea Bocelli, Jovanotti, Eros
  Ramazzotti. Rap and trap (e.g. Marracash, Sfera Ebbasta) are fast and full of slang: save them for
  the Song Workshop later.
