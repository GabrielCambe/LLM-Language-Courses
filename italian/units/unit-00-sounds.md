# Unit 0: Sounds and spelling · CEFR A0 · ~5 days

> Italian is the language in this repo whose sounds are **closest to Portuguese**: the same 7
> vowels, the *nh* and *lh* sounds, and even the "tch" of *tchau* (which comes from Italian *ciao*).
> A Brazilian doesn't have to learn a new mouth. The job is different: **drop 5 Brazilian habits**
> and **learn 3 new things** (the c/g rule, a few spellings, and double consonants).

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can read any Italian word aloud from its spelling (Italian spelling is almost 100% regular).
- I can hear and say double consonants: *pala / palla*, *capello / cappello*.
- I can catch the 5 Brazilian-accent habits and fix them.

> 🔤 **How to read the respellings below:** they use **Portuguese spelling** (*tch, dj, nh, lh, k*),
> with the stressed syllable in CAPS. Every vowel is said in full: *te* is "te", never "tchi".

## 📐 The rule

### Vowels: the same 7 as Portuguese (good news)
Stressed vowels in Italian are the ones you already have:

| Italian | Sounds like the Portuguese… | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | *a* in *casa* (always open, even before m/n) | *casa, mano, banana* |
| **è** (open e) | ***é*** in *café* | *è* (is), *bello, festa, caffè* |
| **é** (closed e) | ***ê*** in *você* | *perché, sera, verde* |
| **i** | *i* in *vida* | *vino, amico* |
| **ò** (open o) | ***ó*** in *avó* | *però, cosa, porta* |
| **ó** (closed o) | ***ô*** in *avô* | *sole, nome, dove* |
| **u** | *u* in *luta* | *uno, luna* |

⚠️ **The accent marks are swapped compared with Portuguese.** In Italian the **grave** accent (è, ò)
is the **open** sound (PT *é, ó*), and the **acute** (é) is the **closed** one (PT *ê*). Italian only
writes them on a **stressed final vowel**, so most of the time you just hear them. Open vs closed
also changes from region to region, so it almost never causes a misunderstanding. Don't lose
sleep over it.

### The two big unlearnings: full vowels, no nasal vowels
1. **No vowel reduction.** Brazilian Portuguese weakens unstressed vowels: *leite* → "leitchi",
   *muito* → "muitu". Italian never does. A final **e stays e** and a final **o stays o**:
   *latte* = "LAT-te", *molto* = "MOL-to", *sette* = "SÈT-te".
2. **No nasal vowels.** *bene* is "BÈ-ne", never "bẽi". *tanto* is "TAN-to" with a plain open *a* and
   a real *n* (tongue touches behind the teeth), never "tãtu". *buongiorno*, *con*, *un*: the n is
   a consonant you can feel.

### Consonants: same letters, different rules

| Spelling | Sound | Portuguese reference | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **c** + e/i | tch | the *tch* of ***tchau*** | *ciao, cena, cinema, dieci* |
| **g** + e/i | dj | the Brazilian *di* of ***dia*** | *gelato, gente, giorno* |
| **ci, gi** + a/o/u | tch, dj (the i is silent: it's a spelling sign) | | *ciao* = "TCHA-o", *giorno* = "DJOR-no", *Giulia* |
| **ch** + e/i | k (**never** the PT *ch*) | the *qu* of ***queijo*** | *che, chi, chiesa, anche* |
| **gh** + e/i | hard g | the *gu* of ***guerra*** | *spaghetti, laghi* |
| **c, g** + a/o/u | k, hard g | as in PT | *casa, gatto, gusto* |
| **sc** + e/i | sh | the ***ch*** of *chá*, the ***x*** of *xícara* | *pesce, scena, uscire* |
| **sc** + a/o/u, **sch** | sk | | *scuola, tedeschi* |
| **gn** | nh | ***nh*** in *banho* | *bagno, gnocchi, signore* |
| **gli** | lh | ***lh*** in *filho* | *figlio, famiglia, aglio* |
| **z**, **zz** | ts or dz (both exist) | the *ts* of *tsunami* | *pizza, grazie* (ts) · *zero, zanzara* (dz) |
| **qu** | always kw | the *qu* of ***quando***, never of *queijo* | *questo* = "KUÊS-to", *qui* = "kui" |
| **s** | s; **z** before b, d, g, l, m, n, r, v | like Brazilian *mesmo* = "mezmo" | *sole, sbaglio* = "zBA-lho" |
| **s** between vowels | z in the North, s in the South: both are correct | | *casa* = "KA-za" or "KA-sa" |
| **h** | always silent (only a spelling sign) | | *ho* = "o", *hanno* = "AN-no", *chi* |
| **r** | the tongue, **always**: start, middle and end of words | the *r* of ***caro***, *prato* | *Roma, porta, per, mare* |
| **rr** | a longer trill (rolled r) | | *terra, carro* |
| **l** | always a clear l, also at the end of a syllable | the *l* of *lua* | *alto* = "AL-to", *il*, *Natale* |
| **t, d** + i | plain t and d | the *t* of *tatu* | *tipo* = "TI-po", *dire* = "DI-re" |

💡 **The mirror image.** Brazilian Portuguese turns *ti/di* into "tchi/dji"; Italian has those exact
sounds but writes them **ci/gi**. So in Italian *ti* (you, *te*) and *ci* (us, there) are different
words. Say *ti* with a Brazilian "tchi" and you've said *ci*.

### Double consonants: the new skill
Italian has **double consonants**, and they change meaning. A double consonant is **held longer**:
your tongue or lips stay closed for a beat, like a tiny pause (the same idea as the small っ in
Japanese). The vowel before it gets a bit shorter.

| single | double |
|---|---|
| *pala* (shovel) | *palla* (ball) |
| *caro* (dear, expensive) | *carro* (cart) |
| *sete* (thirst) | *sette* (seven) |
| *nono* (ninth) | *nonno* (grandpa) |
| *capello* (a hair) | *cappello* (hat) |
| *ano* (anus) | *anno* (year): yes, say *Buon anno!* carefully |

Practise by exaggerating: *fat…to*, *pal…la*, *non…no*. Portuguese spells *rr* and *ss*, but in
Portuguese they're only spelling. In Italian you have to *hear* them.

### Stress
1. Most words: **second-to-last** syllable, as in Portuguese: *CA-sa, a-MI-co, ra-GAZ-zo*.
2. Stress on the **last** syllable is **always written**: *cit-TÀ, per-CHÉ, caf-FÈ, pa-PÀ*.
3. Stress on the **third-from-last** is common and **never marked**: *TA-vo-lo, MAC-chi-na,
   te-LÈ-fo-no, PAR-la-no*. Learn these with the audio.

Watch the cognates: Portuguese *telefone* is te-le-FO-ne, Italian *telefono* is te-LÈ-fo-no.

## ⚠️ Common traps (the Brazilian accent)
- ❌ "LA-tchi", "NOT-tchi" → ✅ *latte, notte*: t stays t, final e stays e.
- ❌ "MOU-tu", "Bra-ZIU" → ✅ *molto* "MOL-to", *Brasile*: l stays l, final o stays o.
- ❌ "bẽi", "tãtu" → ✅ *bene, tanto*: no nasal vowels.
- ❌ "HÔ-ma", "PÔH-ta" → ✅ *Roma, porta*: the Brazilian "h" r doesn't exist in Italian. Use the r
  of *caro* everywhere, and roll it at the start of a word.
- ❌ *chiesa* as "XIÉ-za" → ✅ "KIÉ-za": Italian *ch* is always k.
- ❌ *capello* for "hat" → ✅ *cappello*: a single consonant can be a different word.
- 🇪🇸 **Spanish leaks** (you're studying both): Italian has **no jota** sound, *ll* is just a double
  l (not "y"), and *v* and *b* are different sounds, as in Portuguese.

## 💡 Curiosity
***Ciao*** comes from Venetian ***s'ciavo*** ("(I am your) slave"), a polite "at your service", like
the old Portuguese *seu criado*. Italian immigrants brought it to Brazil, where it became ***tchau***.

And why do *ce, ci* sound "tch"? Classical Latin *c* was always k: *Cicero* was "KI-ke-ro". Over the
centuries, *c* before e and i moved forward in the mouth and became "tch" in Italian (and "s" in
Portuguese: Latin *centum* → Italian *cento* "TCHÈN-to", Portuguese *cem*). That's also why the
Latin course's Italian voice reads *Cicero* as "TCHI-tche-ro": it's reading Latin like Italian.
