# Unit 2: Gender, articles and plurals · CEFR A1 · ~7 days

> Italian has 7 forms of "the" where Portuguese has 4, and its plurals **change the last vowel**
> instead of adding *-s*. Learn every noun **with its article** (not *libro* but ***il** libro*):
> the flashcards always show it.

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can pick the right article (*il, lo, la, l', i, gli, le*) from the start of the noun.
- I can make the plural of most nouns (*il libro → i libri, la casa → le case*).
- I know the nouns whose gender flips between Portuguese and Italian.

## 📐 The rule

### "The": the article depends on how the next word **starts**

| The noun starts with… | m. singular | m. plural | f. singular | f. plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| most consonants | **il** libro | **i** libri | **la** casa | **le** case |
| a vowel | **l'**amico | **gli** amici | **l'**amica | **le** amiche |
| **s + consonant**, **z**, **gn**, ps, y | **lo** studente, **lo** zaino | **gli** studenti, **gli** zaini | **la** scuola | **le** scuole |

The feminine is easy (*la / l' / le*). All the work is in the masculine.
*lo* exists so the mouth doesn't have to say "ilst-": *lo studente*, *lo sport*, *lo zio*, *gli gnocchi*.

### "A, an"

| | before most consonants | before s + consonant, z, gn… | before a vowel |
|---|---|---|---|
| m. | **un** libro | **uno** studente, **uno** zaino | **un** amico (no apostrophe!) |
| f. | **una** casa | **una** scuola | **un'**amica |

### Plurals: change the vowel, no -s

| singular ends in | plural | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-o** (m.) | **-i** | il libro → i libri · il ragazzo → i ragazzi |
| **-a** (f.) | **-e** | la casa → le case · la ragazza → le ragazze |
| **-e** (m. or f.) | **-i** | il fiore → i fiori · la chiave → le chiavi |
| stressed vowel, consonant, or abbreviation | **no change** | la città → le città · il caffè → i caffè · il bar → i bar · la foto → le foto |

Spelling keeps the sound: *l'amica → le ami**ch**e* (k sound), *il lago → i la**gh**i* (hard g). See Unit 0.

### Gender: the ending usually tells you
- **-o** → masculine; **-a** → feminine; **-e** → either: learn it with the article.
- **-ione** → feminine: *la stazione, la lezione, la televisione* (like PT *-ção*).
- **-ore** → masculine: *il fiore, il colore, il dolore* (⚠️ feminine in PT!).
- **-aggio** → masculine: *il viaggio, il messaggio, il coraggio* (⚠️ PT *-agem* is feminine!).
- Exceptions to know: *la mano* (pl. *le mani*), *il problema* (pl. *i problemi*: -ma words from Greek
  are masculine), *la foto*, *la radio*, *l'uomo → gli uomini* (irregular).

### Nouns that flip from Portuguese

| Italian (m.) | Portuguese (f.) |
|---|---|
| il fiore | a flor |
| il colore | a cor |
| il dolore | a dor |
| il viaggio | a viagem |
| il messaggio | a mensagem |
| il ponte | a ponte |
| l'albero | a árvore |

…and the other way round: ***la fine*** (the end) is feminine; PT *o fim* is masculine.

## ⚠️ Common traps
- ❌ *il studente, il zaino* → ✅ ***lo** studente, **lo** zaino*.
- ❌ *i amici* → ✅ ***gli** amici*; ❌ *i studenti* → ✅ ***gli** studenti*.
- ❌ *un'amico* → ✅ *un amico* (apostrophe only for the feminine *un'amica*).
- ❌ *le casas, i libros* → ✅ *le case, i libri*: never add -s.
- ❌ *la viaggio, la colore* (Portuguese gender) → ✅ *il viaggio, il colore*.
- 🇪🇸 Spanish leak: *el, los* → ✅ *il, i / gli*.

## 💡 Curiosity
Why does Italian say *i lupi, le rose* while Portuguese says *os lobos, as rosas*? Both come from
Latin, but from different **cases** (see the Latin course). Portuguese plurals grew out of the Latin
**accusative** (*lupōs, rosās*: the -s survived). Italian plurals grew mostly out of the
**nominative** (*lupī* → *lupi*), which never had an -s.

And *spaghetti* is already plural: one strand is ***uno spaghetto***. Same for *panini* (one *panino*)
and *biscotti* (one *biscotto*). "A panini" in English is like saying "um pães" in Portuguese.
