# Unit 4: Doing things: the present tense (-are, -ere, -ire) · CEFR A1 · ~7–10 days

> This is the tense the exam leans on most: *abito a…, lavoro come…, studio italiano perché…*.
> It's very close to the Portuguese present (*parlo* ≈ *falo*). The work is in 3 small differences:
> the **tu** form, the **noi** form, and **where the stress falls** in the *loro* form.

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can conjugate any regular verb in the present, in all three groups (-are, -ere, -ire).
- I can say where I live, what I do and how often, which is half of the exam's self-presentation.
- I put the stress in the right place: *PAR-la-no*, *A-bi-to*.

## 📐 The rule

### Remove *-are / -ere / -ire*, add the ending

| | **parlare** (to speak) | **prendere** (to take) | **dormire** (to sleep) | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**o** | prend**o** | dorm**o** | falo |
| tu | parl**i** | prend**i** | dorm**i** | falas (você fala) |
| lui / lei / Lei | parl**a** | prend**e** | dorm**e** | fala |
| noi | parl**iamo** | prend**iamo** | dorm**iamo** | falamos |
| voi | parl**ate** | prend**ete** | dorm**ite** | falais (vocês falam) |
| loro | **PAR**l**ano** | **PREN**d**ono** | **DOR**m**ono** | falam |

The three differences from Portuguese:
1. **tu** always ends in **-i**, in all three groups: *parli, prendi, dormi*. No -s, ever.
2. **noi** is **-iamo** for every verb: *parliamo, prendiamo, dormiamo* (Portuguese has *-amos, -emos, -imos*).
3. **loro** keeps the stress where the *io* form has it, so it falls on the **third-from-last**
   syllable: ***PAR**-la-no*, ***PREN**-do-no*, ***DOR**-mo-no*. Never "par-LA-no".

And from Unit 1: **voi** has its own form (*voi parlate*). It's not *vocês falam*: ❌ *voi parlano*.

### The stress stays on the stem in 4 forms
In *io, tu, lui, loro* the stress is on the same syllable of the stem. That's easy for *PAR-lo*, but
watch the verbs where it's further back, the same way the Unit 0 stress rule works:

| infinitive | io | loro |
|---|---|---|
| a-bi-**TA**-re | **A**-bi-to (not "a-BI-to") | **A**-bi-ta-no |
| te-le-fo-**NA**-re | te-**LÈ**-fo-no | te-**LÈ**-fo-na-no |
| or-di-**NA**-re (to order) | **OR**-di-no | **OR**-di-na-no |

*noi* and *voi* move the stress to the ending: *a-bi-TIA-mo, a-bi-TA-te*.

### *-ire* verbs with *-isc-*: *capire* (to understand)
Many *-ire* verbs add **-isc-** in the four stressed forms (*io, tu, lui, loro*):

| capire | sounds like |
|---|---|
| cap**isc**o | "ka-PIS-ko" (sc + o = sk) |
| cap**isc**i | "ka-PI-shi" (sc + i = the PT *ch* of *chá*) |
| cap**isc**e | "ka-PI-she" |
| capiamo | "ka-PIA-mo" (no *-isc-*) |
| capite | "ka-PI-te" (no *-isc-*) |
| cap**isc**ono | "ka-PIS-ko-no" |

The **spelling** stays *-isc-*, but the **sound** changes with the vowel after it: that's the Unit 0
rule (*sco* = sk, *sci* = sh) at work. Common *-isc-* verbs: *capire, finire, preferire, pulire*
(to clean), *spedire* (to send). The dictionary marks them; *dormire, partire, aprire, sentire* don't
take it. There's no way to tell from the infinitive: learn *capisco* together with *capire*.

### Spelling that protects the sound
The same idea, in -are verbs:
- **-care / -gare** add an **h** before *i*, to keep the k / hard g sound: *cercare → cerco, cer**ch**i,
  cer**ch**iamo* · *pagare → pago, pa**gh**i, pa**gh**iamo*.
- **-ciare / -giare / -iare** don't double the i: *mangiare → mangio, mang**i**, mang**i**amo* ·
  *studiare → studio, stud**i**, stud**i**amo*.
- In -ere verbs the sound changes instead: *leggere → leggo* ("LÉG-go", hard g) but *leggi* ("LÉD-dji").

### One present, three meanings (like Portuguese)
*Lavoro* = I work / I'm working / I'll work (soon): *Domani lavoro.* (Amanhã trabalho.)
*stare* + gerund (*sto lavorando* = estou trabalhando) exists too, but only for "right now". Italians
use it less than Brazilians, so the plain present is the safe choice.

### How often: frequency words
*sempre* (always) · *spesso* (often) · *di solito* (usually) · *qualche volta* (sometimes) ·
*non… mai* (never: ***Non** mangio **mai** carne.*, the double negation, like *não… nunca*).

## ⚠️ Common traps
- ❌ *tu parlas, tu prendes* → ✅ *tu parl**i**, tu prend**i***: the tu form has no -s.
- ❌ *noi prendemo, noi dormimo* → ✅ *prend**iamo**, dorm**iamo***: -iamo for everyone.
- ❌ *voi parlano* (from *vocês falam*) → ✅ *voi parl**ate***.
- ❌ "par-LA-no", "a-BI-to" → ✅ ***PAR**-la-no*, ***A**-bi-to*.
- ❌ *io capo* → ✅ *io cap**isc**o* (and *capisci* is "ka-PI-shi", not "ka-PIS-ki").
- ❌ *cerci* → ✅ *cer**ch**i*: without the h it would sound "TCHER-tchi".
- 🇪🇸 Spanish leak: *habláis, vivís* → ✅ *parlate, vivete*; *-amos/-emos* → ✅ *-iamo*.

## 💡 Curiosity
The *-isc-* of *capisco* is an old Latin suffix that meant "**starting to**" do something, and
Portuguese kept it too: it's the *-ec-* of ***florescer*** (to start flowering), ***escurecer***
(to get dark), ***envelhecer*** (to get old). Latin *florescere* gave Portuguese *florescer* and
Italian *fiorire → fiorisco*. In Italian the suffix lost its "starting to" meaning and became a plain
part of the conjugation, but you already use it every time you say *amanhecer*.
