# Unit 1: Ciao! Introductions and *essere* · CEFR A1 · ~5–7 days

> The speaking test of the CILS B1 Cittadinanza opens with a **1-minute self-presentation**,
> answering a list of questions about you. This unit is the start of that minute.

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can greet people at any time of day and say goodbye, formally and informally.
- I can say who I am, where I'm from and what I do, with *essere*.
- I can choose between *tu* and *Lei*, and I know that *voi* is not *vocês*.

## 📐 The rule

### *essere* (to be)

| | essere | Portuguese | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | **sono** | sou | |
| tu | **sei** | és (você é) | |
| lui / lei / Lei | **è** | é | *è* (is) has an accent; *e* (and) doesn't |
| noi | **siamo** | somos | |
| voi | **siete** | sois (vocês são) | |
| loro | **sono** | são | same form as *io sono*: context tells you which |

- **Drop the pronoun**, as in Portuguese: *Sono brasiliano.* Use *io, tu…* only for contrast:
  *Io sono brasiliano, lei è italiana.*
- **Questions** are just intonation, as in Portuguese: *Sei italiano?* No inversion, no extra word.
- **Negation**: *non* before the verb, like *não*: *Non sono italiano.*

### *tu*, *Lei*, *voi*: three kinds of "you"
- **tu**: friends, family, people your age, kids. Verb in the 2nd person: *tu sei*.
- **Lei** (capital L): the **formal** you, for strangers, shop staff, older people, **and the
  examiner**. It takes the **3rd person** verb: *Lei è brasiliano?* Same logic as Portuguese
  *o senhor é* / *você é*: a "you" with a "he/she" verb.
- **voi**: "you all", for any group (formal or not). ⚠️ It has **its own verb form**: *voi
  **siete***. Brazilian *vocês* takes the "they" form (*vocês **são***), so the trap is
  ❌ *voi sono* → ✅ *voi siete*.

### Greetings by time of day

| Italian | When | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|
| *ciao* | hi **and** bye, informal only (tu) | oi / tchau |
| *buongiorno* | hello, from morning until early afternoon | bom dia (+ boa tarde) |
| *buonasera* | hello **and** bye, from mid-afternoon on | boa tarde (tarde) / boa noite |
| *buonanotte* | **only** when going to bed | boa noite (dormir) |
| *arrivederci* | goodbye (works with everyone) | até logo |
| *salve* | a neutral hello, between ciao and buongiorno | olá |

So *buonanotte* is not "good evening": arriving at a dinner, say ***buonasera***.

### Saying where you're from
- *Sono **brasiliano** / **brasiliana**.* Nationalities are lowercase, as in Portuguese.
- *Sono **di** São Paulo.* (city: *di*) · *Vengo **dal** Brasile.* (country: *da* + article)
- ❌ *Sono di Brasile* → ✅ *Sono brasiliano* or *Vengo dal Brasile*.

### Professions
No article, as in Portuguese: *Sono ingegnere. Sono programmatore. Sono studente.*

## ⚠️ Common traps
- ❌ *voi sono* → ✅ *voi siete* (see above).
- ❌ *Lui e italiano* → ✅ *Lui **è** italiano*: the accent is the verb. Without it, it's "and".
- ❌ *Buonanotte!* when arriving at night → ✅ *Buonasera!*
- ❌ *Ciao* to the examiner → ✅ *Buongiorno* / *Buonasera*, and *Lei* throughout.
- ⚠️ ***prego*** is not a nail (PT *prego* = *chiodo*). It means "you're welcome", and also "please,
  go ahead / come in / how can I help?".
- 🇪🇸 Spanish leak: *soy* → ✅ *sono*; *eres* → ✅ *sei*; *usted* → ✅ *Lei*.

  That's how Italians describe someone with *essere*.

## 💡 Curiosity
The formal ***Lei*** is literally "she". It comes from addressing someone as ***Vostra Signoria***
("Your Lordship", a feminine noun), shortened to "she". Portuguese did the same thing with
***Vossa Mercê*** ("Your Grace"), worn down over centuries to ***você***. That's why both *Lei è* and
*você é* use a 3rd-person verb: they started as titles, not as "you". (In 1938 the Fascist
government tried to ban *Lei* as "foreign" and impose *Voi*. It didn't stick.)
