# Unit 2: Gender and articles (and the nouns that flip from Portuguese) · CEFR A1 · ~7 days

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can use *el/la/los/las* and *un/una/unos/unas* correctly.
- I can guess the gender of most nouns from their ending.
- I know the nouns whose gender is different in Portuguese, and why it's *el agua*.

## 📐 The rule

### Articles

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| **the** (singular) | **el** libro | **la** mesa |
| **the** (plural) | **los** libros | **las** mesas |
| **a / an** | **un** libro | **una** mesa |
| **some** | **unos** libros | **unas** mesas |

- Only two contractions: ***a + el = al***, ***de + el = del*** (*voy al cine, el libro del profesor*).
- **No contraction with *en* or *por***: Portuguese *no, na, pelo* → ***en el, en la, por el***.
- **No article before possessives or first names**: *o meu livro* → ***mi libro***; *a Maria* → ***María***.

### Plural
- Ends in a vowel → **-s**: *el libro → los libros*.
- Ends in a consonant → **-es**: *la ciudad → las ciudades*, *el árbol → los árboles*.
- **-z → -ces**: *la nariz → las narices*, *la luz → las luces*.

### Ending rules for gender (≈ 90% reliable)

| Usually **masculine** | Usually **feminine** |
|---|---|
| **-o** (*el libro*) | **-a** (*la mesa*) |
| **-aje** (*el viaje, el mensaje*) · PT *a* viagem! | **-ción, -sión** (*la canción, la televisión*) |
| **-or** (*el color, el dolor, el amor*) · PT *a* cor, *a* dor! | **-dad, -tad** (*la ciudad, la libertad*) |
| **-ma** from Greek (*el problema, el sistema, el idioma*) | **-umbre** (*la costumbre*) · PT *o* costume! |

Famous exceptions: ***la mano*** · ***el día, el mapa*** · ***la flor*** · ***la foto, la moto*** (short for *la fotografía, la motocicleta*).

### *El agua*: the stressed-a rule
Feminine nouns that start with a **stressed *a-* or *ha-*** take ***el / un*** in the singular,
so two a's don't collide: ***el agua, el águila, el hambre, el aula***.
They're **still feminine**: *el agua **fría***, *mucha hambre*, and in the plural ***las** aguas*.

### Nouns whose gender flips from Portuguese

| Spanish | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| **la** leche · **la** sal · **la** sangre · **la** nariz · **la** costumbre · **la** señal | **o** leite · **o** sal · **o** sangue · **o** nariz · **o** costume · **o** sinal |
| **el** árbol · **el** color · **el** dolor · **el** puente · **el** viaje · **el** mensaje · **el** origen | **a** árvore · **a** cor · **a** dor · **a** ponte · **a** viagem · **a** mensagem · **a** origem |

## ⚠️ Common traps
- ❌ *la viaje* → ✅ ***el** viaje* (-aje is masculine)
- ❌ *el leche* → ✅ ***la** leche*
- ❌ *la agua* → ✅ ***el** agua*, but *el agua está **fría***
- ❌ *el mi libro*, *la María* → ✅ *mi libro*, *María*
- ❌ *na mesa*, *no libro* → ✅ *en la mesa*, *en el libro*
- 🇫🇷 **Three-way trap:** French *le lait* (m.), Portuguese *o leite* (m.), Spanish ***la leche*** (f.).
  Spanish is the odd one out.

## 💡 Curiosity
The ***el*** in ***el agua*** isn't really masculine. Old Spanish had a feminine article ***ela***,
which lost its *e* before a vowel: *ela agua → el agua*. Over time it started to look like the
masculine *el*, but the noun never changed gender. That's why the adjective is still *fría*.
