# Unit 0: Sounds and spelling · CEFR A0 · ~5 days

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can read any Spanish word aloud from its spelling (Spanish spelling is almost 100% regular).
- I can tell where the stress falls, and why some words carry a written accent.
- I can catch the 5 Brazilian-accent habits that give me away (and fix them).

## 📐 The rule

### Vowels: 5, always clear
Spanish has **5 vowels: a, e, i, o, u**, and each one always sounds the same.
- No open/closed pairs: there's one *e* (between *ê* and *é*) and one *o*.
- **No nasal vowels.** *pan* is "pan" with a real n, never "pã". *también* ends in a clear n.
- **No vowel reduction:** a final *o* stays *o* and a final *e* stays *e*. *todo* is "to-do", not "todu";
  *noche* is "no-che", not "nochi".

### Consonants: what's different from Portuguese

| Spelling | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **j**, and **g** before e/i | the *jota*: a rough h from the throat | *jirafa, gente, jamón* |
| **g** before a/o/u, **gue/gui** | hard g (the u in gue/gui is silent) | *gato, guerra, guitarra* |
| **güe/güi** | the ü makes the u heard | *pingüino, vergüenza* |
| **c** before e/i, **z** | s in Latin America, *th* in most of Spain | *ciudad, cielo, zapato* |
| **c** before a/o/u, **que/qui** | k | *casa, queso, quiero* |
| **ll**, **y** | y as in "yes" (sh in Argentina and Uruguay) | *llave, yo, calle* |
| **ñ** | nh | *niño, año, España* |
| **ch** | tch, as in *tchau* | *noche, chico* |
| **h** | always silent | *hola, hotel, hay* |
| **b**, **v** | the same sound (soft between vowels) | *vivir, beber, Bolivia* |
| **s** | always s, **never z** | *casa* = "kasa", *presidente* |
| **r** (one, between vowels) | a single tap, like in *caro* | *pero, cara* |
| **rr**, and **r** at the start of a word or after n/l/s | a trill (rolled r, like Italian) | *perro, río, Enrique* |
| **x** | usually ks; in some Mexican names, the jota | *taxi, examen* · *México, Oaxaca* |

### Stress and written accents
Two rules cover every word:
1. Ends in a **vowel, -n or -s** → stress on the **second-to-last** syllable: *CA-sa, HA-blan, LI-bros*.
2. Ends in **any other consonant** → stress on the **last** syllable: *ciu-DAD, ho-TEL, ha-BLAR*.

A written accent (´) marks a word that **breaks the rule**: *ca-FÉ, ÁR-bol, can-CIÓN, LÁ-piz*.
It also tells apart words that sound the same: *sí* (yes) / *si* (if), *tú* (you) / *tu* (your),
*él* (he) / *el* (the). Question words always carry one: *¿qué?, ¿cómo?, ¿dónde?*

Spanish only has the acute accent (´), plus *ñ* and *ü*. No *ã, õ, ê, â, ç*.

## ⚠️ Common traps (the Brazilian accent)
- ❌ "**tchi**empo", "**dji**a" → ✅ *tiempo*, *día*: t and d before i stay t and d.
- ❌ "tod**u**", "noch**i**" → ✅ *todo*, *noche*: final vowels stay full.
- ❌ "ka**z**a" → ✅ *casa*: s between vowels stays s.
- ❌ "Brasi**u**", "hote**u**" → ✅ *Brasil*, *hotel*: final l stays a clear l.
- ❌ *carro* as "ka-ho" → ✅ a rolled "ka-rro". The Brazilian r of *rato* or *carro* is actually
  close to the **jota**: use it for *jamón, gente, ojo*, and roll the tongue for *río, perro, carro*.

## 💡 Curiosity
Medieval Spanish had a "sh" sound, written **x**: *Don Quixote* was "Don Ki-sho-te". In the 1500s–1600s
that sound moved back in the throat and became the **jota**, and the spelling changed to *Quijote*.
That's why *México* is written with an x but said with a jota, and why English still says "Quixotic"
with a ks.

And the **ñ**? Medieval scribes saved space by writing a double *nn* as an *n* with a small *n* on
top, which became the ~: Latin *annus* → *anno* → ***año***.
