# Unit 1: ¡Hola! Introductions and *ser* · CEFR A1 · ~5 days

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can greet people and say goodbye at any time of day.
- I can say who I am, where I'm from and what I do, and ask the same questions.
- I can choose between *tú* and *usted*.

## 📐 The rule

### *Ser* (to be: who or what you are)
Almost the same as Portuguese:

| | Spanish | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|
| I am | **yo soy** | eu sou |
| you are (*tú*) | **tú eres** | tu és |
| he / she is, you are (*usted*) | **él / ella / usted es** | ele / ela / você é |
| we are | **nosotros / nosotras somos** | nós somos |
| you all are (Spain, informal) | **vosotros / vosotras sois** | vós sois |
| they are, you all are | **ellos / ellas / ustedes son** | eles / elas / vocês são |

- **Drop the pronoun.** The verb ending already says who: *Soy Gabriel. Soy de Brasil.* Use *yo*
  only for contrast (*Yo soy de Brasil, ¿y tú?*). Spanish drops it even more than Brazilian Portuguese.
- **No article before jobs:** *Soy programador* (like *sou programador*).
- **Nationality** agrees: *brasileño / brasileña*, plural *brasileños*.

### *Tú*, *usted* and *vos*
- ***tú***: friends, people your age, most everyday situations (more often than you'd think in Spain).
- ***usted***: strangers older than you, formal situations. It's like *o senhor / a senhora*, and takes
  the **él/ella form** of the verb: *¿Usted es de aquí?*
- ***ustedes***: "you all", in Latin America for both friends and strangers (like *vocês*).
  In Spain, *vosotros* is the informal plural. Learn to **recognise** it; you don't need to use it.
- ***vos***: Argentina, Uruguay and much of Central America say *vos* instead of *tú*: ***vos sos***
  de Brasil. You'll hear it in songs and films.

### Greetings

| Spanish | When |
|---|---|
| *hola* | any time, with anyone |
| *buenos días* | until lunch (plural, like *bons dias*) |
| *buenas tardes* | after lunch until dark |
| *buenas noches* | after dark: hello **and** goodbye |
| *adiós* / *hasta luego* / *chao* | goodbye / see you later / bye (Latin America) |
| *¿Cómo estás?* / *¿Cómo está (usted)?* | how are you? |
| *Me llamo…* / *Mi nombre es…* | my name is… |
| *Mucho gusto* / *Encantado, -a* | nice to meet you |

### ¿ and ¡
Questions and exclamations **open** with an upside-down mark: *¿Cómo te llamas?* *¡Hola!*
It tells you how to read the sentence before you reach the end.

## ⚠️ Common traps
- ❌ *Yo soy Gabriel, yo soy de Brasil, yo soy programador.* → ✅ *Soy Gabriel, soy de Brasil, soy programador.*
- ❌ *Bom día* / *Buen día* (Spain) → ✅ *Buenos días* (*buen día* is fine in Argentina).
- ❌ *¿Cómo te llamas?* to your friend's grandmother → ✅ *¿Cómo se llama (usted)?*
- ❌ *Soy brasilero* → ✅ *brasileño* (*brasilero* is colloquial in Argentina and Uruguay).
- 🇫🇷 **French crossover:** French *tu **es*** = Spanish *tú **eres***. Spanish ***es*** = French *il **est***.

## 💡 Curiosity
***Usted*** comes from ***vuestra merced*** ("your grace"), worn down by centuries of fast speech.
Portuguese did exactly the same: ***você*** comes from ***vossa mercê***. That's why both take the
"he/she" form of the verb: you're grammatically talking to someone's *grace*, not to them.
