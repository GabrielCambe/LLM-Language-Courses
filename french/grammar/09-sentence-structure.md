# Sentence structure: how a French sentence is built

> **Big idea:** French word order is basically **Subject → Verb → Object**, like English.
> Almost every surprise comes from the **small words that cling to the verb**: *ne… pas*, object
> pronouns, *y*, *en*. Learn the map below once, and every later rule slots into it.

---

## 1. The sentence map

```
SUBJECT  (ne)  [pronouns]  VERB  (pas/jamais…)  [short adverb]  (past participle)  REST: objects, place, time
```

| Subject | ne | pronouns | conjugated verb | pas | short adverb | participle | rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Je | | | regarde | | souvent | | des films. |
| Je | ne | | regarde | pas | souvent | | de films. |
| Je | ne | **les** | regarde | pas | souvent. | | |
| Je | ne | **les** | **ai** | pas | souvent | regardés | au cinéma. |

**The rule behind the map:** the small words (*ne, pas*, pronouns, short adverbs) cling to the
**conjugated verb**. In the passé composé that's the auxiliary (*ai*, *suis*), not the participle.

**With an infinitive,** pronouns move to the verb they belong to:
*Je vais **le** voir.* (I'm going to see **him**: *le* is the object of *voir*, not of *vais*.)
And *ne pas* sticks together before an infinitive: *Je préfère **ne pas** savoir.*

### 🚫 No "do"
English adds *do* for questions and negation ("**Do** you like it?", "I **don't** know"). French
never does: ***Tu aimes ?*** / ***Je ne sais pas.*** *Faire* means *to make/do* an action,
never a grammar helper.

## 2. The noun phrase: main noun first, details after

English stacks details **before** the noun. French usually puts the **main noun first** and adds
details after it, zooming from general to specific:

| English | French | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| my brother's car | la voiture **de** mon frère | no *'s*: use *de* |
| a coffee cup | une tasse **à** café | *à* = purpose/use |
| a French teacher | un professeur **de** français | *de* = type/content |
| the AI agents meetup | la rencontre **des** agents IA | |
| the little red car | la **petite** voiture **rouge** | BAGS before, other adjectives after |
| the song (that) I love | la chanson **que** j'adore | *que* is never dropped (English drops "that") |

Full template:
**determiner + (BAGS adjective) + NOUN + (other adjectives) + (de/à + noun) + (qui/que clause)**
*la **petite** maison **blanche** de ma grand-mère **que** j'adore*

## 3. Pronoun stacking (A2, Units 17 and 20)

When several pronouns meet, they always line up in this order **before the verb**:

| ① | ② | ③ | ④ | ⑤ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| me, te, se, nous, vous | le, la, les | lui, leur | y | en |

- *Il **me le** donne.* (He gives it to me.)
- *Je **le lui** dis.* (I tell it to him/her.)
- *Il **y en** a.* (There are some.) This chunk is everywhere, so learn it whole.
- *Je **vous en** prie.* (You're welcome / please, go ahead. Formal.)

**Exception: affirmative commands.** Pronouns go *after* the verb, with hyphens, direct before
indirect, and *me/te* become *moi/toi*: *Donne-**le-moi** !* · *Dis-**le-lui** !* · *Vas-**y** !*

## 4. Adverbs

| Situation | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple tense | right **after** the verb | *Je travaille **souvent** le soir.* |
| Compound tense, short adverb (*bien, mal, déjà, souvent, toujours, encore, beaucoup, trop*) | **between** auxiliary and participle | *J'ai **déjà** mangé. Tu as **bien** dormi ?* |
| Time/place adverbs (*hier, demain, ici, là-bas*) | at the **end** (or the start) | *Je l'ai vu **hier**.* / ***Hier**, je l'ai vu.* |
| Long *-ment* adverbs | usually after the participle | *Il a parlé **lentement**.* |

❌ Never between subject and verb as in English "I **often** work": *~~Je souvent travaille~~*.

## 5. Questions (summary; details in [03-negation-questions.md](03-negation-questions.md))

| Register | Word order | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Spoken | statement + rising voice; question word often at the end | *Tu vas **où** ?* |
| Neutral | **est-ce que** + normal order | ***Où est-ce que** tu vas ?* |
| Written/formal | verb–pronoun **inversion** | ***Où vas-tu** ?* |
| Formal with a noun subject | noun + verb–pronoun | ***Marie** parle-t-**elle** français ?* |

## 6. Spoken French: topics first, emphasis by structure

This is the biggest gap between textbook French and the French in songs and films.

### Why French moves words around
English can stress any word with the voice: "**ANGÈLE** sings it" vs "Angèle **SINGS** it".
French stress is fixed at the **end of the phrase** (see Unit 0), so to highlight something
French **restructures the sentence** instead. Linguists describe this as marking the *topic* and
the *focus* (Lambrecht, 1994).

| Device | How | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Left dislocation** | topic first, then a pronoun repeats it | ***Le café**, je **l'**adore.* · ***Moi**, je pense que…* | As for coffee, I love it. |
| **Right dislocation** | pronoun first, topic added at the end | *Il est où, **le bonheur** ?* · *Elle est belle, **ta chanson**.* | Where is it, happiness? |
| **Cleft (*mise en relief*)** | *c'est … qui / que* | ***C'est** Angèle **qui** chante.* · ***C'est** demain **que** je pars.* | It's ANGÈLE who sings. |
| **Presentational *il y a … qui*** | introduce a new person/thing | ***Il y a** quelqu'un **qui** t'attend.* | Someone's waiting for you. |

💡 A sentence like *Moi, mon frère, sa voiture, elle est cassée* ("Me, my brother, his car, it's
broken") is completely natural in speech: it lines up the topics, then makes the statement.
**Recognise it** early; **use it** in conversation from A2. Don't write it in formal texts.

Other spoken patterns you'll meet everywhere: *ne* dropped (*je sais pas*), *on* for *nous*,
*ça* for *cela*, *t'es* for *tu es*.

## 7. Linking sentences

| Level | Connectors |
|---|---|
| A1 | *et* (and), *mais* (but), *ou* (or), *parce que* (because), *alors* (so/then), *aussi* (also) |
| A2 | *donc* (therefore), *puis / ensuite* (then), *quand* (when), *si* (if), *comme* (as/since), *pendant que* (while) |
| B1 | *pourtant* (yet), *cependant* (however), *puisque* (since), *alors que* (whereas), *bien que* + subjunctive (although) |

💡 French schoolchildren memorise the coordinating conjunctions with the sentence
***Mais où est donc Ornicar ?*** ("But where is Ornicar, then?") = *mais, ou, et, donc, or, ni, car*.

## ⚠️ Common traps

| English habit | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| adverb before verb | Je souvent mange. | Je mange **souvent**. |
| object pronoun after verb | Je vois le. | Je **le** vois. |
| possessive *'s* | Marie's book | le livre **de** Marie |
| noun + noun | un café tasse | une tasse **à** café |
| "do" in questions | Fais-tu aimer… ? | Tu aimes… ? / Aimes-tu… ? |
| dropping "that" | La chanson j'adore. | La chanson **que** j'adore. |
| adverb in passé composé | J'ai mangé déjà. | J'ai **déjà** mangé. |
