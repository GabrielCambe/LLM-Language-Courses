# Unit 1: Bonjour! Who are you? · CEFR A1 · ~7 days

## 🎯 Can-do goals
- I can greet someone and say goodbye, formally and informally.
- I can say who I am, where I'm from, and what I do.
- I can choose correctly between **tu** and **vous**.

## 📐 The rule

### Subject pronouns

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **je** (I) | **nous** (we) |
| 2nd | **tu** (you, informal) | **vous** (you plural / you formal) |
| 3rd | **il** (he/it), **elle** (she/it), **on** (one/we, informal) | **ils** (they, m. or mixed), **elles** (they, all f.) |

- **tu vs vous:** *tu* for friends, family, kids, people your age in casual settings. *vous* for
  strangers, older people, shops, work at first. **When in doubt, use *vous*** and wait for the
  other person to suggest *tu* ("*On se tutoie ?*").
- **on** = "we" in everyday speech (*On va au cinéma ?* = Shall we go to the cinema?). It takes the
  **il** form of the verb. Very common in songs.
- **ils** is used for any group with at least one masculine member. 💡 One man in a group of 100 women → *ils*.

### Être (to be): irregular, the most important verb

| | | liaison/notes |
|---|---|---|
| je **suis** | I am | |
| tu **es** | you are | |
| il / elle / on **est** | he/she/one is | *es* and *est* sound the same: "eh" |
| nous **sommes** | we are | |
| vous **êtes** | you are | *vous_êtes* → "voo-zet" |
| ils / elles **sont** | they are | "sohn" (s sound, **not** z) |

### *C'est* vs *il/elle est*
- **C'est** + determiner/noun/name: *C'est Angèle. C'est un ami. C'est une chanson belge.*
- **Il/Elle est** + adjective: *Elle est belge. Il est sympa.*
- **Professions are adjectives here:** *Je suis développeur.* (no "un"!) vs. *C'est **un** développeur.*

### Nationality & origin
*Je suis brésilien / brésilienne. Je viens du Brésil.* (*venir de* comes properly in Unit 9; learn
*je viens de...* as a chunk for now.) Nationalities are **lowercase** in French: *français, belge*.

### 📍 Where it goes in the sentence
**Subject + être + description**: *Je suis développeur.* French has no "do": a question is just *Tu es belge ?*
See the full [sentence map](../grammar/09-sentence-structure.md).

## ⚠️ Common traps
- ❌ *Je suis un ingénieur.* → ✅ *Je suis ingénieur.* / *C'est un ingénieur.*
- ❌ *Il est un bon ami.* → ✅ *C'est un bon ami.*
- Saying *tu* to a shop assistant in France sounds rude. (In Québec *tu* is much more common.)

## 💡 Curiosity
**Stromae** is ***maestro*** with the syllables swapped: *ma-es-tro → stro-mae*. This is
**verlan**, a slang where you flip syllables. The word *verlan* is itself *l'envers* ("the reverse")
flipped. Other common examples: *femme → meuf*, *louche → chelou* (weird), *fou → ouf* (crazy).
You'll hear *c'est ouf !* ("that's crazy!") everywhere.
