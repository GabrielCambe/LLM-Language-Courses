# Latin course: roadmap

**Goal:** read easy, real Latin on your own (adapted texts first, then authors like Caesar and Ovid's
easier passages) in about **6 months** at ~25 min/day.

Latin is learned mostly for **reading**, so this course leans on lots of easy reading
(the method of Hans Ørberg's *Lingua Latina per se illustrata*) instead of memorising
tables in isolation. Classical spelling with **macrons** (ā, ē, ī…) and the restored classical
pronunciation are used throughout.

---

| # | Unit | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pronunciation (classical) | c = k, v = w, ae = "eye", long vs short vowels (macrons), stress |
| 1 | Being: *sum, es, est* | *sum* in the present, questions with *ubi, quis, quid*, word order is free |
| 2 | First declension (*puella*) | Nominative and genitive, singular and plural, masculine exceptions (*nauta*) |
| 3 | Present tense: 1st and 2nd conjugation | *amō, amāre* · *videō, vidēre*, personal endings -ō -s -t -mus -tis -nt |
| 4 | Second declension (*dominus, dōnum*) | Masculine *-us* and neuter *-um* |
| 5 | The accusative | Subject vs object: endings, not word order, tell you who does what |
| 6 | Adjectives: *bonus, -a, -um* | Agreement in gender, number and case |
| 7 | Prepositions | *in, ad, per* + accusative · *in, ā/ab, ē/ex, cum* + ablative |
| 8 | Genitive and dative | "of" and "to/for" |
| 9 | Imperfect and future | *-bam* and *-bō* |
| 10 | Third declension | *rēx, rēgis* · the biggest noun family |
| 11 | Third and fourth conjugation | *dūcō, capiō, audiō* |
| 12 | Perfect tense | *amāvī, vīdī* · the perfect stem |
| 13 | Milestone | Read a whole easy chapter (e.g. Ørberg, *Familia Rōmāna*, ch. I–V) without a dictionary |

## Notes
- 🔊 **Audio:** there are no Latin voices for text-to-speech, so the app uses an **Italian** voice.
  It sounds like Church Latin, not classical: in classical Latin *c* is always "k" and *v* is "w".
- **Portuguese helps a lot:** Portuguese grew out of spoken Latin, so many words are recognisable
  (*aqua* → água, *terra* → terra, *fīlia* → filha). Use that, but watch for false friends.
- **Resources:** Ørberg, *Lingua Latina per se illustrata* (pars I: *Familia Rōmāna*) as the main reader;
  *Wheelock's Latin* as a grammar reference.
